Patient-specific modeling for women’s health

I’m a senior mechanical engineer at Columbia building finite element models of soft tissue in pregnancy — most recently scaling a uterine mechanics pipeline from one patient to five on HPC. Alongside that, reinforcement learning for building controls and a hands-on habit of machining, casting and molding the things I design. I’m looking for R&D work in medical devices and women’s health.

Selected work

  • Multi-patient uterine mechanics

    Scaled a verified single-patient modeling framework to a five-patient cohort. Mean agreement of 3.6 ± 2.2 mm across 580,749 nodes, with boundary conditions transferring between anatomies without re-tuning.

    • FEA
    • Biomechanics
    • FEBio
    • HPC

  • Reinforcement learning for building heating control

    Soft Actor-Critic agents holding a comfort band in FMU co-simulation, stabilized with TD3 techniques to replace bang-bang switching with smooth modulation.

    • Reinforcement Learning
    • Controls
    • PyTorch

  • Cast and injection-molded model car

    One object, two tooling routes: a silicone-cast body from 3D-printed molds, and injection-molded wheels from CNC-machined aluminum.

    • Manufacturing
    • CNC/CAM
    • Injection Molding
