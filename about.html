About

I’m a senior at Columbia studying mechanical engineering, graduating in May 2027.

Most of my work sits at the point where a physical system is too complicated to reason about without a model, and too consequential to model carelessly. In pregnancy that gap is wide: only about 5% of births arrive on the estimated date, preterm birth affects roughly a tenth of births worldwide, and the mechanics regulating labor onset are difficult to observe directly in a patient. Finite element modeling is one of the few ways to study that environment without touching anyone, which is what drew me to it and why I want to keep working on it.

The other half of how I work is in the shop. I machine, cast, mold and print the things I design, and that habit changes how I model — it is harder to accept a clean simulation result after watching a mold leave its layer lines on every part that comes out of it, or a printed bearing wobble a mechanism out of sync. Both halves point at the same question, which is whether a result describes the thing or describes the method used to get it.

I’m looking for R&D roles in medical devices and women’s health, and I’m happy to talk about any of the work here.

What I’m working on

In the Computational Biomechanics Research Lab under Prof. Vijay Vedula, I scaled a patient-specific uterine mechanics pipeline from a single-patient proof of concept to a five-patient cohort, running nonlinear finite element jobs on HPC and verifying a fast Robin boundary condition model against a contact-based FEBio reference. That work is written up here.

This year I’m also on a five-person senior design team building an assistive menstrual disc inserter — a device that lets people with limited hand dexterity or mobility place a disc independently. We’re translating user needs into design requirements and early SolidWorks concepts now, and it will go on this site once there is something built to show.

Before that I spent a year in the Building Energy Research Lab designing Soft Actor-Critic agents for building heating control, validated in FMU co-simulation.

Industry experience

I interned at Nanov Display Inc. in spring 2025, designing weather-resistant outdoor kiosks and transit LCD and LED signage in SolidWorks and AutoCAD, and releasing the drawings and renderings that went with them. I set up new displays end to end — all electrical and PCB-level wiring, embedded system configuration on Windows and Ubuntu, and factory acceptance testing with the compliance documentation behind it.

It was a small Brooklyn office and I was the only engineer handling that side, so I also ran technical-support meetings with suppliers, worked with them to bring BOM cost down, and hosted visiting clients through the warehouse and kiosks. That work is covered by confidentiality, so none of it appears on this site — but it is where I learned what it takes to release a drawing someone else has to build from, and I’m glad to talk about the engineering in general terms.
